How Verdict AI works

From "they're speaking" to "point scored" — in under a second.

Say something true and the screen flashes green before you've finished your next breath. Say something false and it flashes red. Nobody's raising a hand to fact-check you — it's already done. Here's the machinery behind that, round by round.

1 Setup

Six formats, one screen. Pick Public Forum and the whole speech order snaps into place — constructives, crossfires, summaries, final focus, real time limits, zero guessing. Parliamentary runs the actual PMC → LOC → MG → MO → LOR → PMR sequence. Casual mode throws all of that out — you just set the rounds, the clock, and whether sides trade off.

Pick a topic — a preset or your own — name the debaters, set your spoken language, decide what points are worth, and choose whether an AI judge weighs in at the end.

2 Live speech capture

Hit start and the mic goes live. Words show up almost as fast as you say them — faded at first, while the recognizer's still deciding what it heard, then locked in solid once it's sure. That mic light isn't decoration; it's the only proof anyone's actually listening.

And while you're mid-sentence on your next point, the one you just finished is already getting checked behind the scenes.

3 Claim detection & fact-check

Every sentence has to clear two questions before anything happens:

  1. Is there an actual factual claim here? (Opinions, questions, filler, and predictions get skipped — you won't get dinged for saying "I think.")
  2. Is that claim true or false, based on well-established public knowledge?

The checks are paced on purpose — they won't stack up mid-speech, they won't double-check something you already said, and they go quiet during crossfire, because even we can't tell who's talking over whom.

4 Instant feedback

The verdict comes back fast, and it only goes three ways:

  • Verified — screen flashes green, a short high tone plays, and the speaker gets +fact.
  • False — screen flashes red, a low tone plays, and the speaker loses −wrong.
  • Unclear or not really a claim — a banner briefly shows what it found, but no points move.

A banner surfaces the claim in plain English, one line of reasoning, and whoever just got scored. Every one of these calls is tagged · auto in the undo tray, in case the machine got it wrong and you need to say so.

5 Manual scoring & fallacies

The AI doesn't get the final word — you do. Missed a claim? Hit +fact or −wrong yourself. Heard a straw man or a slippery slope slide by? The −fallacy button pulls up all 16 — pick the one that fits and it docks the point and tags the transcript right where it happened.

Or skip the button entirely — highlight the phrase yourself in the live subtitles and a "Fact-check this" shortcut pops up, running the exact same check on demand.

6 End of debate

When the clock runs out — or you end it yourself — everything lands on Results: final scores, the fact / wrong / fallacy tally for each side, a highlight reel of every moment that got flagged, and the full transcript, ready to export.

Turn on AI Judging and you also get an actual verdict — a points-based one that just does the math, or a substance-based one that reads the whole transcript and calls who made the stronger argument.

· Privacy

Your audio never leaves your browser. What gets sent out is the text of the one sentence being checked, nothing more — not a recording, not the round before it, not your name. Nothing is stored anywhere afterward. And if the fact-checker can't reach its engine for any reason, it doesn't throw an error at you — it just goes quiet and hands control back, and the app keeps running in fully-manual mode like nothing happened.

· When to overrule the AI

It will get things wrong sometimes — statistics, very recent events, and anything that hinges on exact wording are where it stumbles. It's tuned to stay quiet unless it's genuinely confident, but "genuinely confident" still isn't "certain." Watch the banner. If a call looks off, it probably is — undo is one click away in the tray, every time.
